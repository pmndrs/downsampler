# downsampler vs webgpu-spd 3.0.0

A side-by-side test of [webgpu-spd](https://github.com/JolifantoBambla/webgpu-spd) (`JolifantoBambla`), a WGSL port of AMD FidelityFX SPD that uses SPD's single-dispatch atomic handoff. It covers correctness against this repository's CPU oracles, performance with the SPEC §8 method, and features. Everything was measured on one device (Apple M5 Pro) on 2026-11-12.

**Summary**
- **Its handoff produced wrong levels on this device.** Whenever a pass generates more than 6 mips, which is its default when the device allows more storage textures, the last workgroup reads mip 6 that other workgroups wrote with plain stores. In 256 back-to-back frames:
  - Up to 5 % of frames had stale levels 7+ at render sizes (rgba8unorm, rgba16float).
  - At 2048² it was 4–18 %, at 4096² 25–41 %, and at 256² 34–77 %. 256² is the only requested size it reduces in one dispatch.
  - With 6 mips per pass (no handoff) and with r32float (mip 6 in a read-write texture), no frame was wrong.
  - This is the failure SPEC §2.2 predicts from the WGSL memory model. It is now observed, and it explains [webgpu-spd#7](https://github.com/JolifantoBambla/webgpu-spd/issues/7).
- **Without the handoff it matches our SPD oracle:** 0 mismatches on every in-bounds level at every size, for both formats. Its odd-size results are SPD's, so its min/max pyramids are not conservative: at render sizes, 0.07–52 % of texels miss a base texel in their footprint. Ours has none under `'exact'`.
- **It is not faster here.**
  - Against our `'exact'` at render sizes, its default is 1.14–1.41× in rgba16float (0.95× at 1707×960) and 1.16–1.32× in rgba8unorm. Its handoff-free 6-mip mode is 0.81–1.25×.
  - Our `oddSizes: 'spd'` has the same dispatch count and the same semantics. It is 0.50–0.85×, so 1.2–2.1× faster than webgpu-spd's handoff-free mode.
  - Its single dispatch (256², 8 mips) measured 0.91× of `'exact'` for rgba8unorm, against 0.90× for our `'spd'` in 2 dispatches.
  - Its subgroup path was slower than its own non-subgroup path on this device.
- **It does some things we don't:** integer formats, rgb10a2, sub-rectangles, a 4-way custom filter, a smaller bundle, and one simple call. It has also been released for 1.5 years. We do things it doesn't: render-pass-matching and conservative odd sizes, tool hooks, sums and weights, a scalar reduction, depth sources, rgb9e5ufloat, compatibility mode, any size, and no reliance on unguaranteed memory ordering.

## Device and method

- **Device:** Apple M5 Pro, Metal 3, headless Chrome (puppeteer 24).
  - Features requested when available: `texture-formats-tier1`, `bgra8unorm-storage` and `timestamp-query`, plus `subgroups` and `shader-f16`, which webgpu-spd uses.
  - Not requested: `texture-formats-tier2`. So webgpu-spd keeps mip 6 for its handoff in its own f32 storage buffer for rgba8unorm and rgba16float, and in a read-write storage texture for r32float.
  - `maxStorageTexturesPerShaderStage` is the adapter's: **8**. Subgroup size is 32.
- **webgpu-spd variants**
  - `wspd`: its default on this device. That is 8 mips per pass (`min(limit, 12)`), with the atomic last-workgroup handoff for levels 7–8 and subgroup quads.
  - `wspdNoSg`: the same, with `disableSubgroups`.
  - `wspd6`: 6 mips per pass, so no handoff. Its own `setPreferredLimits` requests this limit (it caps the request at 6, line 1283).
  - Each variant has its own `WebGPUSinglePassDownsampler`. Every texture uses a cached `SPDPass` from `preparePass`, as its README recommends for per-frame use.
- **Dispatch counts.** With 8 storage textures, no requested render or square size is one dispatch for webgpu-spd. 960×540 … 4096² have 9–12 mips, so `wspd` runs 8 levels plus the rest, and `wspd6` runs 6 plus the rest: 2 dispatches each.
  - Only chains of ≤ 8 levels run in one dispatch, so 256² was added.
  - A one-dispatch 4096² needs a device with 12 storage textures.
- **Correctness:** `npm run build && node test/run.js --webgpu-spd-check --skip-validation --frames=256`. Code in `test/bench/webgpu-spd.js`; raw results in `webgpu-spd-check.json`.
  - **Fixtures:** averages for rgba8unorm and rgba16float (random; HDR [−4, 12) for rgba16float), and min/max for r32float (random [0, 1)). All in place, with every level poisoned first.
  - **Oracle:** each webgpu-spd pass is checked against SPD semantics, computed from the GPU's own pass source level (the stored level its dispatch reads). That means ⌊n/2⌋ sizes, the last row/column dropped, no rounding inside a pass, and each level quantized as stored.
    - On levels whose blocks lie inside the source, this equals our `spdReference`, whose clamp never triggers there.
    - Tolerances are our declared `tolerance()`: 2.01 steps for unorm8, max(0.002, 0.3 %) for float16, exact for folds.
  - **Unit-step levels** are those from the first 2:1 step along an axis that is already 1 texel. That is level 10 at 1129×635, 1280×720 and 1707×960; the other sizes have none.
    - These levels read outside their source. WGSL lets that return zero or any in-bounds texel.
    - So they are compared with three models: zero (0,0,0,0), zero with alpha 1, and clamp-to-edge.
  - The same levels are also compared bit for bit with our `oddSizes: 'spd'`, which has the same semantics through our `'clamp'` kernel.
  - **Hi-Z:** for every max (min) level, we count texels smaller (larger) than some base texel inside their normalized footprint (`maxViolations`). The comparison is our `'exact'`.
  - **Missing or stale levels:**
    - For each size, format and variant, 256 frames cycle through 4 pre-uploaded inputs.
    - Each frame copies an input into level 0, poisons levels ≥ 5 with a buffer copy, runs the pass, and copies levels ≥ 5 out.
    - This runs twice: once with every frame in one encoder per chunk of 32, and once with one submit per frame, as an app records frames.
    - Every frame must equal, byte for byte, the majority of three isolated runs of that input, with the queue idle between runs. The isolated runs are also compared with each other.
  - **Probes** for questions that reading the code raised: separate-target mip count, `halfPrecision`, the shared mip-6 buffer, sub-rectangles, and 8192².
- **Performance:** `npm run bench -- --compare=webgpu-spd --formats=rgba8unorm,rgba16float,r32float --skip-validation`, using the SPEC §8 method. Raw results in `webgpu-spd-{1,2,3}.json`.
  - Timestamp markers bracket 16 repeats; marker cost is subtracted. 11 rounds with rotated order, after warm-up.
  - **Parity check before timing:** each webgpu-spd variant is compared with our `'spd'` (averages) or with `wspd6` (max) on every level above its first unit step.
    - Levels 1–6 must match, and did.
    - Mismatches from level 7 on depend on the handoff. They are recorded, not fatal, because they occurred (see Correctness).
    - `'exact'` and the tail kernel must match the render baseline.
  - **Cells:** the median over 3 runs of each run's median **paired per-round ratio**, to `'exact'` for averages or to `'exact'` max for max. Under 1 is faster. Dispatches are in brackets. The ratios' spread across runs was within ±0.05 in most cells (JSON).
- **Load.** The machine is shared, and other agents run GPU suites on it.
  - Load averages (1/5/15 min): correctness 7/19/207 → 6/10/120. Bench runs 11/10/110 → 19/12/107, 20/12/107 → 16/12/105, 16/12/105 → 13/12/102.
  - Only same-run ratios mean anything. The `'exact'` column's ms are given only to show scale.

## Correctness

### Levels against the oracle (single run per size, all levels)

- **In-bounds levels, all variants, both formats, all 9 sizes:** 0 mismatches beyond tolerance and 0 poisoned texels. The only exception is 256² rgba16float, where `wspd` had 2 and `wspdNoSg` 6 values off by up to 0.06. Those are levels 7–8, the handoff's.
- **Exact matches:** 97.6 % (rgba8unorm) and 97.8–97.9 % (rgba16float) of values equal the oracle bit for bit. The rest are one rgba8 step or one f16 ulp: tie rounding of the GPU's f32 → unorm8 store, and Metal's truncating f16 store (SPEC §6.1).
- **Against our `oddSizes: 'spd'`:** 97.4–97.9 % bit-identical, at most 1 rgba8 step or 0.0078 apart (rgba16float).
- **Unit-step levels** (level 10 of 1129×635, 1280×720 and 1707×960; 1×1):
  - They match the clamp-to-edge model in every case and neither zero model. That is consistent with Dawn's robustness clamping out-of-bounds texture loads on Metal.
  - They are within 0.8 % (rgba8unorm) and 0.4 % (rgba16float) of our `'spd'`, which clamps per block.
  - On a backend that returns zeros, which WGSL also allows, the same 1×1 would average two real values with two zeros: half the true mean. AMD's SPD has the same exposure (SPEC §6).

### WGSL memory-model reliance (line refs into `src/index.ts`)

- **Payload:** mip 6 is written with a plain store to `mip_dst_6_buffer` (22, declared non-atomic at 129), or with `textureStore` to a read-write texture.
- **Handoff:**
  - Invocation 0 takes a ticket with `atomicAdd` on `spd_global_counter` (127, 167).
  - The workgroup runs `storageBarrier()` (191) and reads the ticket with `workgroupUniformLoad` (171).
  - The workgroup holding the last ticket resets the counter (`atomicStore`, 175, 554), optionally runs `textureBarrier()` (510), then reads every workgroup's mip 6 with plain loads (32).
- **Why WGSL doesn't make that safe:** "All atomic built-in functions use a relaxed memory ordering … No synchronization or ordering guarantees apply between atomic and non-atomic memory accesses." All synchronization functions "use the Workgroup memory scope" (WGSL §Atomic built-in functions, §Synchronization built-in functions). So the ticket says nothing about the visibility of other workgroups' payloads.
  - Our own atomic probe (`test/bench/atomic-spd.js`) passed its checks because its payloads go through `atomicStore`/`atomicLoad` on single words. Same-location atomics are coherent in practice, but the ordering is still not guaranteed.
  - webgpu-spd's payloads are plain stores, and they failed.
- **Subgroups:** quad swaps in compute (202–204), the 64 → 8×8 lane remap (`map_to_xy`, 91), and `local_invocation_index % 4 == 0` as the quad leader (297, 340, 373, 413, 444). Together these assume quads are lanes 4k…4k+3 and that the lane index is `local_invocation_index`.
  - WGSL defines quads only for fragment shaders and says "There is no defined relationship between subgroup values … and `local_invocation_index`."
  - It produced correct levels 1–6 here (0 mismatches).
- **Out-of-bounds reads** at unit-step levels: implementation-defined (above).

### Probes (`probes` in the JSON)

- **Separate target, sized as in the README** (`mipLevelCount = maxMipLevelCount − 1`): `numMips` defaults to `target.mipLevelCount` (1384), and `preparePass` generates `numMips − 1` levels (1104). So **the last target level is never written** (still poisoned).
  - Passing `numMips: maxMipLevelCount( size )` writes it.
- **`halfPrecision` does not work.** `sanitizeScalarType` selects f16 only when `'shader-f16'` is *not* enabled (818).
  - With the feature enabled, the option is a silent no-op: bit-identical to f32, no warning.
  - Without the feature, it warns "falling back to full precision" and then emits `enable f16;`, which fails shader creation ("extension 'f16' is not allowed in the current environment").
  - The f16 shader would not compile anyway: it declares `spd_intermediate_bg` (157) but uses `spd_intermediate_ba` (216, 227). Its counter buffer is also sized for ⌈layers/2⌉ (1036).
- **The shared mip-6 buffer leaks between textures.** One counter buffer and one 64×64 mip-6 buffer serve every texture of a device and layer count, and each dispatch rewrites only its own tiles.
  - **Strip:** a 2048×64 strip's level 7 reads entries no workgroup of its dispatch writes. It came out 64 or 191 depending on whether the previous 4096² texture was black or white.
  - **Sub-rectangle:** a sub-rect update of a white 1024² texture (rect 512² at the origin, after a black texture ran) produced **48 of 64 level-7 texels from the other texture**.
  - Sub-rects also don't regenerate levels below the rect's own log2 size (1384).
- **Above 4096²:** with 8 mips per pass, 8192²'s mip 6 is 128² but the last workgroup reduces one 64×64 tile, and the buffer index overflows its 64-wide rows.
  - **3/4 of levels 7–12 were never written** (3072 of 4096 at level 7), and level 13 was wrong.
  - With 6 mips per pass, 8192² is fine.

What the numbers say (M5 Pro only):
- **Same structure, our kernel is faster.** Our `'spd'` and `wspd6` both take 2 dispatches with SPD semantics.
  - Dividing the two columns: ours is 1.2–2.1× faster at render sizes (rgba8 1.5–2.1×, rgba16f 1.2–1.7×) and 1.5–2.0× at 1024². They tie at 4096².
  - For averages, our first level is one bilinear sample per output texel. webgpu-spd's is four `textureLoad`s and an f32 average, and its workgroup keeps four separate per-channel arrays in workgroup memory.
- **The handoff costs more than the dispatch it removes.** At render sizes `wspd` (8 + rest) is 1.1–1.25× of `wspd6` (6 + rest), with the same dispatch count, and 1.1–1.2× at 1024².
  - This matches SPEC §2.2: every workgroup pays a storage barrier and an atomic, and one workgroup does the tail serially.
  - Where it does remove a dispatch (256²), `wspd` is 0.91× and `wspd6` 1.00×, while our 2-dispatch `'spd'` is 0.90×.
- **Its subgroup path is slower here:** `wspd` (subgroups) is 1.05–1.36× of `wspdNoSg` at render sizes and 1.15–1.25× at 1024². Quad swaps replace workgroup-memory reductions, but they move four `vec4f` per level and gain nothing on this GPU.
- **Against our default `'exact'`,** webgpu-spd's default is slower nearly everywhere: 1.16–1.41× at render sizes (0.95× at 1707×960 rgba16f) and 1.5–2.0× at 1024².
  - `'exact'` pays 5–8 dispatches at render sizes for render-pass-matching results, and still wins or ties.
  - Its handoff-free mode is 0.81–1.25×.
- **Max:** webgpu-spd is faster than our `'exact'` max at most render sizes (0.46–1.05× for `wspd6`). The structural reason is that `'exact'` folds take 5–8 dispatches there. But the result isn't a valid Hi-Z (52 % violations at 1129×635).
  - Our conservative alternative at SPD-like cost is a `Reducer` with `'zero-weight'` edges; that wasn't timed here.
  - At 1024²–4096², where both are exact and conservative, webgpu-spd ties or is up to 2.3× slower; at 256² its single dispatch is 0.68–0.91×.
- **Absolute stakes are small.** The largest gap at a render size is ~25 µs per chain on this loaded machine.

| **Reductions** | average, min, max, minmax (r → rg), custom `spd_reduce_4(v0, v1, v2, v3)`. | **Formats** | 16 core, incl. integer rgba8/16/32 uint/sint, r/rg32 uint/sint, rgba8snorm. 23 more with tier1, incl. **rgb10a2unorm/uint**, rg11b10ufloat, 16-bit norm. bgra8unorm with its feature. ## Pros and cons

**webgpu-spd: what it does better**
- **Simpler to adopt:** one `generateMipmaps` call, a small API, a hosted demo and docs. It is released and versioned.
- **Formats we lack:** integer formats (with a declared truncating average), rgb10a2, and the full tier1 list.
- **Sub-rectangle updates** (but see the probe for the buffer path).
- **A 4-way custom filter,** which can express weighted kernels that our binary fold can't under `'exact'` (our `Reducer` covers them with sums and weights).
- **Smaller:** 8.3 KB gzipped against 13.9 KB for our `Downsampler`.
- **One dispatch for short chains** on devices with enough storage textures. It is also SPD-familiar for users porting from D3D12/Vulkan.
- **Handoff-free mode is sound:** with `maxMipsPerPass: 6` (or a device limited to 6), results match SPD semantics and no stale frames appeared.
- **Its `SPDPass.encode( computePass )`** fits engines that own their passes, as our `encodePass` does.

**webgpu-spd: what to watch for**
- **The default is unsafe** wherever the device exposes more than 6 storage textures (most devices: ~94 % report ≥ 8). Stale levels 7+ appeared in up to 77 % of frames here, and isolated runs disagreed. **Set `maxMipsPerPass: 6`**. It cost nothing here: `wspd6` was as fast or faster in every row except 256², where the single dispatch saves 9–12 %.
- **The shared mip-6 buffer** makes deep levels depend on other textures (strips, sub-rects), and it breaks above 4096².
- **SPD odd sizes:** non-conservative min/max (unsuitable for Hi-Z culling at render sizes) and implementation-defined 1×1 levels at some sizes.
- **Smaller bugs:** `halfPrecision` is broken, a separate target's last level is unwritten by default, and MinMax clamps max at 0.
- **Not available:** compatibility mode, depth sources and tool hooks.
- **Not faster** than our kernels on this device in any mode.

**downsampler: what it does better**
- Results that WGSL guarantees.
- Default odd sizes that match render-pass/WebGL mipmaps, with conservative min/max for Hi-Z.
- The fused-pass tooling: load/store hooks, sums and weights, level selection, `ScalarReduction`.
- Depth sources, rgb9e5ufloat, compatibility mode, any size.
- 1.2–2× faster than webgpu-spd at render sizes for the same semantics (`'spd'`) on this device. Even `'exact'` with 5–8 dispatches beats or ties webgpu-spd's default.

**downsampler: cons**
- Unreleased.
- More API surface.
- No integer, rgb10a2, sRGB or sub-rectangle yet.
- A larger bundle.
- Its `'exact'` default needs 5–9 dispatches at render sizes (cheap here, unmeasured on other vendors).
- No single-dispatch mode for those who want SPD's structure literally.

## Which to pick

- **Hi-Z / occlusion culling from depth, or anything that must be conservative:** ours (`'exact'`), or webgpu-spd only at power-of-two sizes.
- **Mip chains that must match render-pass mipmaps (three.js, engines switching paths):** ours (`'exact'`).
- **Tools fusing a reduction into their own pass (FSR-style luminance pyramids, exposure, denoiser weights):** ours (`Reducer`, `ScalarReduction`).
- **Integer textures, rgb10a2, or sub-rectangle updates today:** webgpu-spd, with `maxMipsPerPass: 6`. On the read-write texture path that WGSL guarantees no more than the buffer path, so stay on 6 mips per pass there too.
- **A small drop-in for colour mips where SPD's odd-size behaviour is acceptable:** either. webgpu-spd is smaller and simpler (again with `maxMipsPerPass: 6`); our `oddSizes: 'spd'` was faster here.

**Not measured:** other GPUs (NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, mobile), other browsers, devices with ≥ 12 storage textures (true single-dispatch 4096²), `texture-formats-tier2` (read-write mip 6 for rgba8/rgba16f), and webgpu-spd's sub-rect timing.
